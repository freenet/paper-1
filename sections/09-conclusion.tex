\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Applications on Freenet define their state algebra in sandboxed WebAssembly. A contract-supplied summary/delta protocol synchronizes that state across a mesh of neighboring co-hosts, while demand-gated subscription leases keep hosting aligned with active use. The underlying small-world ring protects a nearest successor and predecessor while shaping its remaining links through gap-based \textsf{CONNECT} targeting and Kleinberg-style acceptance scoring. Forwarding ranks neighbors by expected cost, predicted from an isotonic distance prior with per-neighbor and per-region corrections weighted by the evidence behind them. Delegates isolate private state and sensitive operations behind a platform-enforced trust boundary.

This architecture targets decentralized group chat, collaborative documents, social feeds, reputation systems, and other applications with mergeable state. Several mechanisms remain unexercised at scale, experimental, or unimplemented. In particular, evaluating asymptotic scaling will require measurements across network sizes rather than further design analysis alone.
